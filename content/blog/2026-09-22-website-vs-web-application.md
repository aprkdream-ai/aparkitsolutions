"We need a website" can mean very different things. Sometimes it means a fast, professional site that explains your services. Other times it means a customer portal, booking system, or dashboard. Those are web applications, and they have different costs, timelines, and technical requirements.

Knowing the difference helps you brief developers accurately and budget realistically.

## What is a website?

A website primarily **presents information**. Visitors read about your company, services, and articles, and contact you through a form. Most of the content is the same for every visitor.

Typical features:

- Service and company pages
- Blog or news section
- Contact and enquiry forms
- Search-engine optimisation and analytics

## What is a web application?

A web application lets users **do things**: log in, create and manage records, make bookings, pay, or collaborate. What each user sees depends on who they are and what data they have.

Typical features:

- User accounts, roles, and permissions
- Databases and business logic
- Dashboards and reports
- Integrations with payments, CRMs, or other systems

## Key differences at a glance

| | Website | Web application |
|---|---|---|
| Main purpose | Inform and generate enquiries | Let users complete tasks |
| User accounts | Rarely | Usually |
| Complexity | Lower | Moderate to high |
| Ongoing maintenance | Light | Regular |

## Which one do you need?

Choose a **website** if your goal is to present your business, build trust, and generate leads.

Choose a **web application** if customers or staff need to log in, manage information, or complete workflows online.

Many businesses need **both**: a marketing website that explains the offer and a web application that delivers the service. These can share a brand and domain while being built and maintained separately.

## Planning for growth

If you expect to add application features later, mention it at the start. A developer can then structure hosting, domains, and design so the transition is smooth rather than a rebuild.
