Choosing who builds your software is one of the most important decisions in a technology project. The right partner helps you clarify what to build, delivers it predictably, and leaves you with a product you can maintain. The wrong one can leave you with missed deadlines, unclear costs, and code nobody wants to touch.

Here is a practical checklist to use when you compare development partners.

## 1. Do they start with the problem, not the technology?

A good partner asks about your business goals, users, and current processes before recommending a framework or quoting a price. Be cautious if a proposal jumps straight to features and technology without showing that the team understands *why* the software is needed.

Look for:

- A discovery or requirements phase, even if it is short
- Questions about users, success criteria, and constraints
- Willingness to suggest a smaller first release

## 2. Is the delivery process transparent?

You should know how work is planned, how progress is reported, and when you will see working software. Iterative delivery, with regular demos on a staging environment, lets you catch misunderstandings early, when they are cheap to fix.

Ask how they handle:

- Change requests and re-prioritisation
- Progress updates and who your point of contact is
- Testing and quality assurance before each release

## 3. Who owns the code and infrastructure?

Confirm in writing that you will own the source code, documentation, and accounts for hosting, domains, and third-party services. Ownership protects you if you later bring development in-house or change partners.

## 4. How do they approach security?

Security should be part of design and development, not a final checklist. Ask about authentication, input validation, data protection, dependency updates, and how secrets such as API keys are stored.

## 5. Can they show relevant experience?

Relevant experience matters more than a long client list. Ask to walk through a comparable project: the problem, the approach, the trade-offs, and what they would do differently. Specific, honest answers are a good sign.

## 6. What happens after launch?

Software needs updates, monitoring, and improvements. Clarify support options, response times, and how ongoing work is billed *before* the project starts.

## A simple scoring approach

When comparing proposals, score each partner from 1 to 5 on understanding of the problem, clarity of process, communication, technical approach, ownership terms, and post-launch support. Price matters, but the cheapest quote rarely produces the lowest total cost.
